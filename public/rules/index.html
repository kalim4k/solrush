SolRush rules

Two players, one board, and a simple choice every turn: run, or build. Here is everything the game asks you to know, and the handful of ideas that decide most matches.

SolRush is Quoridor played in the browser. You have a pawn on a 9×9 board and a supply of walls. Your pawn starts on your own back row; your goal is the row on the opposite side. So does your opponent's, in the other direction. Whoever crosses first wins, immediately.

Your turn, in one sentence

On your turn you do exactly one of two things: move your pawn one square, or place one wall. You may not do both, and you may not pass.

Moving your pawn

Your pawn moves one square up, down, left or right. Never diagonally, and never through a wall.

Jumping over your opponent

If your opponent is standing on the square you wanted, you do not lose the move — you jump straight over them and land on the square immediately behind.

If that landing square is unavailable — a wall stands behind them, or they are on the edge of the board — you may instead step diagonally around them, to either side. This is the one time a pawn moves diagonally.

Walls

A wall is two squares long and is placed between rows or columns rather than on a square. Three things follow from that, and they matter more than they sound:

  • A wall blocks both players equally. There are no walls of your own colour. Anything you build is also in your way.
  • A wall is permanent. Once placed it stays for the rest of the game. Nothing removes it, including you.
  • Your supply is limited. Spend your last wall and you can only move for the remainder of the game, whatever happens next.

Walls may not overlap, and they may not cross another wall at its centre.

The rule that decides most games

You may never seal your opponent off completely. After every wall you place, both players must still have some route to their goal row — however long you have just made it.

A wall that would trap someone is simply refused: nothing is placed, and nothing is spent. So you cannot win by walling someone in. You can only make their journey longer than yours.

Once that lands, the whole game changes shape. It stops being about blocking and becomes a race where you are allowed to lengthen the other lane.

Winning

SolRush has two modes, and they are won differently.

Duel — the classic
You start on opposite sides of a 9×9 board and race to reach the other's back row. Ten walls each.
Race
You both start at the bottom of a taller 9×13 board and run for the same top row. Fifteen walls each. Because you travel together rather than past each other, walls interfere far more often.

In both, the first pawn to reach its goal row wins the moment it arrives.

The clock

Each player has a time bank for the whole game, and a separate limit of 30 seconds for any single move. Running out of either one loses the game.

The 30-second rule applies even in rooms created with no time limit — otherwise a player who walks away from their phone leaves the other waiting forever.

If your opponent loses their connection the clock stops, and they have a minute to come back before the game is awarded to you. Locking your screen or switching apps mid-game does not forfeit the match.

Strategy for new players

The rules take two minutes. These take rather longer, and they are the difference between a beginner and somebody who is difficult to beat.

  1. Count the race before you build. The entire game is a comparison of two shortest paths: yours and theirs. Before spending a wall, ask whether you are already ahead. If you are, walking forward wins — and costs nothing.
  2. An early wall is usually a wasted wall. On move three your opponent has the whole board to go around it. The same wall placed twenty moves later, when their route has narrowed, can cost them five squares.
  3. Good walls lengthen; they do not block. You are not allowed to seal anyone off, so stop looking for the wall that stops them. Look for the one that forces a detour and hands you the tempo.
  4. Remember that your walls trap you too. A surprising number of losses come from a wall the player built themselves and then had to walk around.
  5. Keep two walls for the end. A player with walls left has answers. A player with none can only run, and their opponent knows exactly where they are going.
  6. Use the jump. Jumping over an opponent who has stepped in front of you gains a square you would otherwise have spent a whole turn recovering. In a close race that single square is the game.

Common questions

Do I need an account?
No. You can play against the AI, or online, without signing up. An account exists so your rating, streak and history follow you between devices.
Is it free?
Yes, the whole game is free to play.
How do I play a specific friend?
Create a room and send them the code. They enter it and you are both in the same game, wherever they are.
Does it work offline?
SolRush installs as an app and games against the AI work with no connection. Playing other people needs one, naturally.
Is this the same as Quoridor?
Duel is Quoridor, played online. Race is a variant on a taller board where both players run in the same direction.
